## What was established

### New Section 5 of Part IV (`theory/mass-gap-strong-coupling.tex`, now 16 pages)
The upper bound now holds on the whole range $0<\beta<\beta_0(d)$, by a different proof:

* **Positivity** (Lemma `mg:lem:pos`, every $\beta>0$, every finite periodic lattice). The one-link operator of the transfer
  matrix splits as $k_\beta=c_0\Pi_0+k'_\beta$ with both parts positive ([F] `lem:kop`, $c_r>0$). Expanding the tensor product
  over the four links of $p_0$ writes $\hat T=\sum_F\hat T_F$ with every $\hat T_F\ge0$ and commuting with the gauge projection.
  So $\langle(B(0)-b)(B(1)-b)\rangle$ is a sum of sixteen non-negative traces, and keeping one of them gives
  $\Cov(A(0),A(1))\ge\langle(A-a)(A_1-a)\prod_{s\in X_0}\varphi_\beta(U_s)\rangle$ with $\varphi_\beta=1-c_0/w_\beta$: the four sides of
  the unit cube are forced to carry non-trivial characters. (A form of reflection positivity for the reflection through
  $x_0=\frac12$.)
* **Exact cube** (Lemma `mg:lem:char`): for real inversion-invariant class functions $a_0,a_1$,
  $\int a_0(U_{p_0})a_1(U_{p_1})\prod_sf_\beta(U_s)=\sum_{r\ne0}\hat a_{0,r}\hat a_{1,r}u_r^4$ (all terms $\ge0$ when $a_0=a_1$); and
  $\int|\varphi_\beta|\le e^{4\beta}Nu/\sqrt{\tau_0}$.
* **Five plaquettes** (Lemma `mg:lem:five`, purely combinatorial): if every plaquette of $Y\ne\emptyset$ has at most one link in
  $B$ and every link of $E(Y)\setminus B$ is in at least two plaquettes of $Y$, then $|Y|\ge5$ (sharp: cube caps).
* **Two-point bounds** (Lemma `mg:lem:twopoint`): the loop expansion of the local expectation sums exactly over subsets of the
  cube (common partition-function ratio, normalised by the expansion of $\langle1\rangle$), and every other term has $\ge5$
  plaquettes outside the cube, so $\Cov\ge\tau_0u^4L(\beta)$ and $\Var\le\tau_0V(\beta)$ with $L,V=1+O(\beta)$ explicit.
* **Upper bound** (Theorem `mg:thm:upper`): $m\le4\log(1/u)+D(\beta)$, $D=\log(V/L)=2(1+2/\sqrt{\tau_0})\beta+O(\beta^2)$, in infinite
  volume and for every spatial lattice with sides $\ge3$, whenever $L>0$. Lemma `mg:lem:range`: on the whole range,
  $L>0.9969-8.2\cdot10^{-4}N^4/\tau_0^2$, so for $N\le3$ (e.g. $U(1)$, $SU(2)$, $SU(3)$): $L>0.73$ and $D<0.32$.
* **Corollary `mg:cor:twosided`**: for $N\le3$ and all $0<\beta<\beta_0(d)$, $4\log(1/q(\beta))\le m(\mu_\beta)\le4\log(1/u(\beta))+D(\beta)$.

Numbers (`theory/checks/upper-bound/constants_upper.py`): the maximum of $D$ on $(0,\beta_0(d))$ is $4.8\cdot10^{-3}$ ($SU(2)$),
$4.9\cdot10^{-2}$ ($SU(3)$), $3.8\cdot10^{-3}$ ($U(1)$) in $d=3$ and $1.4\cdot10^{-3}$, $1.0\cdot10^{-2}$, $1.3\cdot10^{-3}$ in $d=4$.
So on the whole range the upper bound is $4\log(1/u)$ to within a few per cent, against $\beta\lesssim10^{-16}$ before.

### Section 6 of Part IV: temporal plaquettes at their operator norm (refereed)
In the transfer-matrix representation of a term $\Phi_F(X)$ of the duplicated expansion, the temporal plaquettes of a layer form the operator
$\bigotimes G_g\otimes\bigotimes\Pi_0\otimes\Pi_0$ composed with the gauge projection. Here $G_g=F_f\otimes\Pi_0+\Pi_0\otimes F_f+F_f\otimes F_f$ and
$F_f=\sum_{r\ne0}u_r\Pi_r$, so $\norm{G_g}=u_*=\max_{r\neq0}u_r$, against the sup norm $e^{4\beta}-1$ used before. All layers but one are bounded in operator
norm, and one in trace norm (Lemmas `mg:lem:layerop`, `mg:lem:transfer`). Hence $m\ge4\log(1/q_u)$ with $q_u=eKe^{2(K+1)\theta}u_*$
(Theorem `mg:thm:sharp`), in infinite volume and in finite volume with spatial sides $\ge4$. Moreover $u_*=u$ for $\dim\rho\le49$ (Corollary
`mg:cor:window`), so the window between the two bounds shrinks from about $35$ ($SU(2)$, $d=4$) to $4\log(eK)$ as $\beta\to0$ ($16.0$ for $d=4$,
$13.9$ for $d=3$; at most $24.0$ on the range), independently of $G$. At $\beta_0(4)$ for $SU(2)$: new lower bound $16.68$, old $5.59$, Part II
$5.89$, upper bound $40.67$.

### Section 7 of Part IV: the sharp constant by counting fronts (referee pending)
- **Fronts.** A connecting set $X$ is organised by its fronts (the components of the temporal plaquettes of one layer) and its clusters (the
  spatial plaquettes of one slice). A front changes only where a cluster or a support touches it (column lemma and persistence). So $X$ is a
  union of segments (unchanged fronts over time intervals) and clusters, with an incidence graph that is connected and contains a path from

## Part 4: the first-order constant ("keep going on the first-order constant")

### Idea
Section 7's $\delta\approx2\cdot10^4\beta$ ($d=4$) came from counting a one-plaquette cluster next to a four-edge front together with any front
touching it. In fact almost every such configuration has a free link. The remedy is to stop treating clusters and fronts as separate vertices of
the path. Instead:
- **Junctions.** Group everything that meets at one slice: the markers there and the fronts of the two adjacent layers, connected by touching
  (the components of a graph $H_s$).
- **Junction graph.** Its vertices are junctions and segments, each segment joined to its two end junctions.
- **Split weights.** The weight $a^{|\sigma|\ell}$ of each segment is split as $a^{|\sigma|/2}$ at each end junction. On the path, a segment then
  carries exactly $(za^{|\sigma|})^\ell$, and every non-path front of a junction carries $a^{|\psi|/2}\approx a^2$.
- **Local constraint.** In a support-free junction every edge satisfies $n_J(e)\ge2$: the two layers plus the plaquettes of the junction's
  clusters. This one condition contains both the column lemma and closedness.
- **What survives.** Exactly one junction of order $b$ lets a four-edge front through: the **cap**, a plaquette closing the front at a slice where
  it continues. With two plaquettes and a four-edge exit there are only the tube shift and the tube jump: $T_v$ junctions, of order $b^2$. In
  addition:
  - an exit at the entry's layer (a "U-turn") needs at least three plaquettes;
  - an exit with 5 edges needs at least four;
  - three plaquettes give a structured count;
  - extra fronts cost $a^2$.

## Part 5: the cap by characters ("keep going on the cap by characters")

### A correction to open thread 1 of Part 4
Part 4 said that the cap's first-order matrix element vanishes for $SU(N)$. That holds for the matrix element between tubes of the same
orientation, $\int|\chi_\rho|^2\chi_\rho=$ (multiplicity of $\rho$ in $\rho\otimes\rho$) $=0$. But the cap also joins a tube carrying $\rho$ below to
one carrying $\bar\rho$ above (relative to a consistent orientation of the surface). That matrix element is $\int\chi_\rho^3$, the multiplicity of
the trivial representation in $\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\rho$. It is $1$ for $SU(3)$, where three fundamental fluxes can meet along an edge (the
determinant). So the first-order term vanishes for $SU(2)$, $U(1)$ and $SU(N)$ with $N\ge4$, but not for $SU(3)$.

### What was established (Part IV, new Section 9, commit `ee48f88`; 44 pages)
* **The number.** $\kappa(\beta):=\tau_0^{-1}\int(\mathrm{Re}\,\chi_\rho)^2f_\beta\,dU$ is the norm of the cap restricted to the plaquette states
  $\chi_\rho,\chi_{\bar\rho}$, attained at $\mathrm{Re}\,\chi_\rho$. Also $\kappa=\frac\beta{N\tau_0}\int(\mathrm{Re}\,\chi_\rho)^3+O(\beta^2)$, with an
  explicit remainder (Lemma `mg:lem:capelement`).
  * The cubic integral vanishes when the centre acts on $\rho$ by a scalar that is neither $1$ nor a cube root of unity.
  * For $SU(3)$ it is $\frac14$, by an elementary invariant-tensor proof, so $\kappa=\beta/6+O(\beta^2)$.
* **Transfer bound by pieces** (Lemma `mg:lem:pieces`). Write $\mathcal K=\tilde{\mathcal K}^{1/2}\mathbf P\tilde{\mathcal K}^{1/2}$ and regroup the
  trace as $\Tr\prod_s\mathbf P\mathcal O_s\mathbf P$. At each slice $\mathbf P\mathcal O_s\mathbf P$ factorises over the pieces (the junctions and
  the untouched fronts), each with the gauge projections at its own vertices. This is Lemma `mg:lem:transfer` split exactly into the weights of
  Section 8, so that one factor can be computed.
* **The cap by characters** (Lemma `mg:lem:capchar`). The gauge-projected cap is $\Lambda M_g\Lambda$ on the class functions of $(U_q,U'_q)$. Its norm
  is at most $u^4\bar\kappa_{\rm cap}$, with $\bar\kappa_{\rm cap}=\kappa+(2-\iota)N^2u^2+O(\beta^3)$. The term $(2-\iota)N^2u^2$ is a "copy switch" of
  the duplicated system: the tube changes copy at the cap.
* **Lower bound** (Theorem `mg:thm:caplower`): Theorem `mg:thm:junctions` with $b\to b_{\rm cap}$ in the cap term of $\Sigma_4$, plus a second fix.
  * The fix: for exits with $k\ge5$ edges, $\sum_\ell(za^k)^\ell\le a^{k-4}/(1+\delta'-a)$, not $a^{k-4}/\delta'$. The condition becomes
    $P_v(g_CY_1/5+g_FY_0)<F(1+\delta'-a)$.
  * Without the fix $F\sim b/\delta'$, and the six-edge exits leave $\delta/\beta^2\approx4.2\cdot10^3$.
  * Result: $\delta_\chi=\kappa+(2-\iota)N^2u^2+(4P_v-4-K_S)\gamma_g^2+O(\beta^3)$.
* **Upper bound** (Theorem `mg:thm:upperchar`). In Lemma `mg:lem:twopoint` two sup-norm bounds become identities: $M_{S_A}(A^2)=\tau_0(1+\kappa)$ and
  $\hat a_\rho=\tau_0(1+\kappa)-aNu$.
  * So $D^\chi=-\log(1+\kappa)+2\eta_2+O(\beta^3)$; for $SU(2)$ this is $\frac38\beta^2$, where before it was $6\beta$.
  * Whole range: $D^\chi\le-\log(1+\kappa)+D_0$ with $D_0$ increasing.
* **First-order term** (Corollary `mg:cor:firstorder2`): $m(\mu_\beta)=4\log(1/u)-\kappa(\beta)+O(\beta^2)$, also in finite volume (sides $\ge4$).
  * $SU(2)$, $U(1)$, $SU(N\ne3)$: $4\log(1/u)+O(\beta^2)$.
  * $SU(3)$: $4\log(1/u)-\beta/6+O(\beta^2)$.
