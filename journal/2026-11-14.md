## Part 19: the third-order term of SU(5) ("keep going on the third-order term")

**Established.**
1. *Group data from exact Haar moments* (`sun_group.py`; Numerical, exact rational coefficients).
   * By Schur–Weyl duality, $\int\chi^a\bar\chi^b=\sum f^\lambda f^\mu$ over partitions with at most $N$ rows that give the same $SU(N)$ representation.
   * So $u$, $U_\pm$ and $\Upsilon$ are power series in $\beta$, evaluated at any precision (with extra digits at small $\beta$, where $\Upsilon-1=O(\beta^3)$).

2. *Lemma 16.11 (Theorem), the second character coefficient of $SU(N)$.*

## Part 20: the third-order term as a computer-assisted theorem ("keep going on the third-order term")

**Why this, and not $d=5$.** With only the general-$d$ machinery, $D/\beta^4\approx10^{11}$ at $\beta_0(5)=8.06\cdot10^{-5}$ for both groups, against $28.7$ ($SU(3)$) and $2.07$ ($SU(5)$). That is where
$d=4$ stood before Section 16's counts. So $d=5$ means redoing the whole counting programme with larger enumerations, over several sessions, in a dimension with no physical role. The
weaker point of what was done is its status. Every whole-range result (Remarks 16.7(vii), (x), 16.9, 16.12) was Numerical, for three reasons:
* the evaluation at $\beta_*$ is in floating point, with truncated series;
* the monotonicity facts behind the scaling were checked on a 400-point grid, a gap every referee noted;
* the counts come from computer enumeration.

The first two can be removed.

**Established.**
1. *Lemma 16.13(i) (Theorem):* $e^{-2\beta}\le\Upsilon\le e^\beta$ for every compact $G$ and irreducible $\rho$, since the tilted weight $w_\beta/c_0$ lies in $[e^{-2\beta},e^\beta]$ relative to Haar measure.
2. *Lemma 16.13(ii) (computer-assisted):* $\Upsilon\ge U_+\ge1$ for $SU(5)$ on $(0,4.03\cdot10^{-4}]$. Write $U_+-1=P/Z^2$ with $P=2Z_2Z-2Z_1^2-Z^2$. The coefficients of $P$ are exact rationals to order 30,
   with $p_3=\frac1{48}$, and the rest is bounded by majorants, as a sum of non-negative terms with no cancellation: $P\ge t^3(\frac1{48}-5.6\cdot10^{-11})$. A first version subtracted two numbers
   near 1 and got a "negative tail" of rounding noise; it was rewritten.
3. *Bound mode* (`rigorous_bounds.bound_group`, `--bounds`). The lower bound's hypotheses depend on the group only through $u,u_2,\sigma_6,\Upsilon,\bar\Upsilon$, monotonically. Replace them
   by $\bar u$ and $u_{\rm low}$, $u_2$ of Lemma 16.11, the bound on $\sigma_6$, $e^{-2\beta}\le\Upsilon\le e^\beta$, and $\Upsilon\le\bar\Upsilon\le\Upsilon+\Delta$:
   * $\bar\Upsilon\to e^{-2\beta}$ on the right sides and in $C_\varpi$;
   * $\bar\Upsilon\to e^\beta+\Delta$ in the cost terms;
   * $\log(\bar\Upsilon/\Upsilon)\le e^{2\beta}\Delta$ in the deficit.

   Each bound is $\beta^k$ times a visibly increasing function, so every monotonicity fact of the scaling holds by inspection. The deficits barely move: $2.555$, $0.3456$, $3.935$, $0.5192$
   against $2.554$, $0.3449$, $3.932$, $0.5187$ with the exact data.
4. *The scaling in bound mode*, from $\beta_*=\beta_0(d)(1+10^{-30})\ge\beta_0(d)$ (an earlier $d=4$ base point lay $4\cdot10^{-14}$ below $\beta_0(4)$). All 17 values of $t$ pass:
   * $SU(3)$: $D\le2.5576\beta^4$ ($d=3$), $3.9369\beta^4$ ($d=4$);
   * $SU(5)$: $0.34598\beta^4$ ($d=3$), $0.51941\beta^4$ ($d=4$).
5. *The error budget* (`error_budget.py`). Every truncated series at $\beta_*$ has a geometric tail bound, using Lemma 16.10 for fronts and binomial majorants for clusters and polymers: at most
   $1.4\cdot10^{-21}$ relative. With the tails, the decoration and barred inequalities hold with room $\ge6.7\cdot10^{-16}$, the marked systems have $\det\ge0.57$, and the rest have room

   digits. Upper bounds without the 11-plaquette count, using the binomial bound instead: $E=0.0415$, $0.0469$ ($SU(3)$) and $0.3178$, $0.3603$ ($SU(5)$) (`certify_upper.py`).
7. **Theorem 16.14 (computer-assisted).** For $SU(3)$ and $SU(5)$ in $d=3,4$, in infinite volume and for all $\beta<\beta_0(d)$: $-c_-\beta^4\le m-4\log(1/u)+\log\Upsilon\le c_+\beta^4$, with
   $(c_-,c_+)=(2.558,0.042)$, $(3.937,0.047)$, $(0.346,0.318)$, $(0.520,0.361)$. The third-order term ($\beta^3/432$, $-\beta^3/6000$) is determined from both sides on the whole range.
