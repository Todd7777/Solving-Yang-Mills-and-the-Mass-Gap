## Part 13: the next order of the third-order term at practical β ("keep going on the third-order term")

**Starting point** (Part 12). With Lemma 16.4 the lower bound resolved the $SU(3)$ third-order term $\beta^3/432$ for $\beta\le2.4\cdot10^{-6}$ ($d=4$). For $d=3$
the paper said $\beta\le2.6\cdot10^{-5}$. The second referee had noted that the exactly counted part of $M_5$ is negligible: the gain comes from where the
remainder starts (its corners), not from the counts.

**What was established.**
1. *A correction (the criterion).* The lower bound gives $m-4\log(1/u)+\log\Upsilon\ge-D$ with $D=8(K+1)\theta_7+\log(\bar\Upsilon/\Upsilon)+\log(1+\delta')$. The thresholds of
   Remark 16.5 must compare $D$, not $\delta'$ alone, with $\beta^3/432$.
   * In $d=4$ at $2.4\cdot10^{-6}$ the $\theta_7$ term is $0.3\beta^4$, so the old value stands.
   * In $d=3$ the $\theta_7$ loss grows like $\beta^8$. At $2.6\cdot10^{-5}$ it is $47\beta^4$, and $D=131\beta^4>89\beta^4$, so the claim $2.6\cdot10^{-5}$ was wrong. The correct
     threshold, with the counts below, is $2.3\cdot10^{-5}$.
   * The first referee had included $\theta_7$; the threshold taken from the second report had not, and I did not check. `practical_beta.py` now prints $D$ and its
     three parts.
   * $\theta_7$ alone exceeds $\beta^3/432$ for $\beta\gtrsim2.9\cdot10^{-5}$ ($d=3$) and $\gtrsim1.2\cdot10^{-5}$ ($d=4$).
2. *Fronts by a decision search* (`fronts_decision.py`). All edges at one vertex are decided at a time, so each front is produced once. It takes seconds where our
   Redelmeier code took hours. It confirms $N^v_9=4344$ ($d=4$, the referee's value) and gives $N^v_{10}=43650$ ($d=4$), and $N^v_{11}=1728$, $N^v_{12}=6369$ ($d=3$).
3. *Exact two-plaquette exits* (`zexit_count.py`). By Lemma 8.6(i) the exit lies in $\partial q\cup E(P)$, so a search over the subsets of at most 12 links is complete:
   * $Z_{2,k}=4,4,22,36,42,24,18$ ($d=3$) and $20,12,182,296,606,456,458$ ($d=4$), for $k=6..12$;
   * against crude counts $\approx1.7\cdot10^7$, which made up most of the "exits" term at $\beta\approx10^{-5}$.
4. *Lemma 16.6 (Theorem): junction sums by corners.* For any finite set $S$ of exponents $(n,E)$, closed downwards, the pairs in $S$ may be counted (or bounded), and the rest
   is bounded from the minimal exponents outside $S$. Lemma 16.4 is the case $S_5$.

## Part 14: further orders in d=3 ("keep going on the third-order term")

1. *Automatic classes.* `order_counts.py D m` finds all junction classes with a pair in $S(m)=\{n+E\le m+\frac12\}$ and counts them. For $m=6,7$ it
   reproduces the hand-made lists exactly. For $d=3$, $m=8$ there are 22 classes and 34 kinds of pairs (21 min).
2. *Exact exits with $n\ge3$* (`zexit_count_n.py`). The exit lies in $\partial q\cup E(P)$, so the sets $P$ joined to $q$ by vertex chains are enumerated, then the fronts in
   that small graph. $Z_{3,k}$ reproduces the earlier 20, 20, 46. $Z_{4,k}$ and $Z_{5,k}$ are at most 3672 and 38064, against crude binomials.
3. *Closed sets up to 14 plaquettes in $\mathbb Z^3$:* 2, 10, 11, 24, 0, 70. The extending search now remembers only closed sets, and the third referee and we agree on
   $\mathbb Z^4$: 60, 66 with 10, 11.
4. *Order 8 in $d=3$:* resolved for $\beta\le3.2\cdot10^{-4}\approx\beta_0(3)/1.26$ ($D=6.76\beta^4<7.23\beta^4$; not at $3.3\cdot10^{-4}$). Without the exact exits it was $3.1\cdot10^{-4}$.
5. *Order 9 in $d=3$, in part* (Remark 16.7(vii)). Of the 36 classes of order 9, the twelve with a pair at $n+E\in\{9,\frac{19}2\}$ and $n\ge4$ were counted:
   seven with `order_counts_par.py` (with a cache), five (the classes with four front-pairs, and with fronts of $6,6$ and $6,7$ edges) separately. The classes with two or
   three plaquettes have fronts of 12 to 15 edges and are slow: a deficit/capacity pruning saved about 25% and an index filter was slower. So they are left out, which
   Lemma 16.6 allows: $S''_9=S(8)\cup\{n+E\le\frac{19}2,\ n\ge4\}$, corners $(2,7),(3,6),(5,5),(6,4),(7,3),(8,2)$. Leaving them out costs little: $D/\beta^4=2.55$
   at $\beta_0(3)$ instead of $2.36$ (the placeholder test with all of order 9 at $10^8$ each). New counts:
   * $(4,5)$: $2432$ (fronts $4,4,10$), $2\cdot168$ ($4,4,4,6$), $2\cdot64$ ($4,6,6$, $p'$ of six edges), $276$ ($4,4,7$, $p'$ of seven edges);
   * $(4,\frac{11}2)$: $4556$ ($4,4,11$), $2\cdot168$ ($4,4,4,7$), $128$ ($4,6,7$, $p'$ of six edges);
   * $(5,4)$: $1152$ ($4,4,8$), $3\cdot4$ ($4,4,4,4$); $(5,\frac92)$: $2288$ ($4,4,9$); none at $(6,3)$, $(6,\frac72)$, $(7,2)$.

   `orders/assemble_d3_s9.py` rebuilds `orders/d3_s9.json` from `orders/d3_m8.json` and these counts, and checks that the twelve classes are all that have such a pair,
   each counted far enough, and that the corners are the minimal exponents outside $S''_9$.
6. *The whole range in $d=3$ (Numerical; `whole_range_d3.py`).* With $S''_9$, $D/\beta^4=2.55$ at $\beta_0(3)$ against $\beta^3/432=5.75\beta^4$; the majorant is
   $2.35\beta^4$ of it. On a grid of 22 points from $10^{-12}$ to $\beta_0(3)$, $D(\beta_{i+1})<\beta_i^3/432$ for all neighbours (largest ratio $0.53$, on
   $[3.8\cdot10^{-4},\beta_0(3)]$). Between grid points: with the parameters of $\beta_{i+1}$ fixed ($\varpi$, $\bar b$, $\bar a$, $F$, $F_6$, the decoration constants), every
   sum in the hypotheses and in $\delta'$ increases with $\beta$ and drops at least like $\beta/\beta_{i+1}$, while the right sides change only through $\bar\Upsilon=1+\beta/6+\dots$; so
   the same $\delta'$ stays admissible below $\beta_{i+1}$, and as $8(K+1)\theta$ and $\log(\bar\Upsilon/\Upsilon)$ increase with $\beta$ (checked on 402 points), $D(\beta)\le D(\beta_{i+1})$.
   So the lower bound resolves the $SU(3)$ third-order term for all $10^{-12}\le\beta<\beta_0(3)$ in $d=3$. Below $10^{-12}$ this was at first only asymptotic
   ($D\le(8u_1^4+\varepsilon)\beta^4$ for small $\beta$, Theorem 14.2); item 8 closes it.
7. *$d=4$:* the next obstacle besides the majorant is the crude $Z_{n,4}$, $n\ge8$, which exceeds $\beta^3/432$ near $2\cdot10^{-5}$. Order 7 in $d=4$ is being counted; the
   first classes reproduce $N_3=876$, $N_4=42$, $N_{3,6}=680$, $N_{3,7}=344$, $N_{2,8}=992$ and give, e.g., $912$ junctions with five plaquettes and fronts $4,4,4$,
   $18276$ and $17028$ with four plaquettes and fronts $4,4,6$ and $4,4,7$, $9220$ with three plaquettes and fronts $4,4,8$.
8. *Scaling in $\beta$: the whole range* (Remark 16.7(vii), first committed with the grid only). For $\beta=t\beta_*$ take $\varpi=t^p\varpi_*$, $F=(1+\kappa)tF_*$,
   $F_6=(1+\kappa)tF_{6*}$, $\delta'=(1+\kappa)^2t^4\delta'_*$ and the same $\xi_F,\xi_C,h_J,\bar b,\bar a$, with $\kappa=\beta_*$. Every term of the hypotheses has at least the order in $t$ of its
   side ($t^4$ in $\Sigma^{(4)}_4$, $t$ in the conditions on $F$ and $F_6$), the decoration conditions improve ($C_\varpi\sim t^{-p}$), and the factors $1+\kappa$ absorb the decrease of
   $\bar\Upsilon=1+\beta/6+\dots$ on the right sides. The monotone quantities ($a/\beta$, $b/\beta$, $\epsilon/\beta$, $b_2/\beta^2$, $\sigma_6/\beta^6$, $\theta/\beta^6$, $\log(\bar\Upsilon/\Upsilon)/\beta^4$) were checked at 302
   points from $10^{-24}$ to $\beta_0(3)$. Hence $D(\beta)/\beta^4\le(1+\kappa)^3D(\beta_*)/\beta_*^4$ for $\beta\le\beta_*$. `scaling_check.py` (with a new option `fixed` of `lower3e`, which
   evaluates every hypothesis, including those on $\xi_F,\xi_C,h_J$, at given parameters) confirms it at 17 values of $t$ from 1 to $10^{-20}$ for $\beta_*=\beta_0(3)$: all admissible,
   $D/\beta^4\le(1+\kappa)^3D(\beta_*)/\beta_*^4=2.5574<5.749$. **So the lower bound resolves the $SU(3)$ third-order term on the whole range $0<\beta<\beta_0(3)$ in $d=3$** (Numerical). The same scaling justifies
   "$D/\beta^4$ increases with $\beta$" (up to $(1+\beta)^3$), which the earlier thresholds had used without proof.

9. *The upper bound does not see the third-order term at practical $\beta$* (Remark 16.7(viii), `upper_third_order.py`). Its error $D_2+\log\Upsilon$ is $O(\beta^5)$ with
   constant $2.6\cdot10^{12}$ ($d=3$) and $5.2\cdot10^{13}$ ($d=4$), so it stays below $\beta^3/432$ only for $\beta\le3.0\cdot10^{-8}$ and $6.7\cdot10^{-9}$. Almost all of it is the tail
   $\mathcal T_Q$ of Lemma 5.5, at $\beta_0(3)$ $5.2\cdot10^9\beta^4$, with $\mathcal T_p$ ($5.4\cdot10^8\beta^4$) next: crude rooted counts $(1+\frac1K)^{J'}(eK)^n$ of the relatively closed
   sets of Lemma 5.4, activity $\epsilon^n$. So far the third-order term is seen from below only. A rough estimate: exact counts of the relatively closed sets up to
   about 10 plaquettes (for $S_Q$) and 8 (for $S_A$) would leave the five-face caps on the faces of $Q$, $N_5\epsilon^5$ times the factor $e^{16\beta}N^4/\tau_0^2=324$ of $L$; with
   activity $\epsilon^5$ that alone exceeds $\beta^3/432$ above $\beta\approx2\cdot10^{-4}$, so the caps also need their character activity (order $u^5$).

## Part 15: the third-order term from above ("keep going on the third-order term")

1. *The upper bound's tails.* Its error at practical $\beta$ was entirely the tails $\mathcal T_Q,\mathcal T_p$ of Lemma 5.5 (item 9 above). Three changes (Lemma 16.8, Theorem):
   * the ratio bound with $\theta_5$ (Lemma 6.1 holds in exactly that setting) instead of $\theta=x/(1-x)$, so $q\approx eK\epsilon$ instead of $\approx x$ (at $\beta_0(3)$: $0.026$ against $0.07$);
   * the admissible sets $Y$ (the sets of Lemma 5.4) counted exactly up to $n_0$ plaquettes, the rooted count only beyond;
   * the five-face caps by characters: integrating the cap's eight inner links gives $\sum_{r\ne0}d_ru_r^5\chi_r(U_{\partial F})$, so its activity is $\sigma_5=\sum d_r^2u_r^5\approx18u^5$
     instead of $\epsilon^5\approx(36u)^5$ (the computation of Lemma 5.1(ii)).
2. *Counts* (`rel_closed_count.py`, Numerical): in $\mathbb Z^3$ up to 10 plaquettes, $S_A$: 2 caps, then $8,0,0,10,47$; $S_Q$: 6 caps, then $12,0,0,30,93$ (cubes sharing a link
   or edge; $2\times1\times1$ boxes; pairs of caps on opposite faces). The rooted count gives $2.5\cdot10^8$ for $n=5$ alone. In $\mathbb Z^4$ up to 8: $S_A$ 4 caps and 32; $S_Q$ 18
   caps, 60, 0, 24.
3. *Result ($d=3$, Numerical).* At $\beta_0(3)$, $\mathcal T'_{S_Q}=1.2\cdot10^{-3}\beta^4$, $\mathcal T'_{S_A}=5\cdot10^{-4}\beta^4$ (from $5.2\cdot10^9$ and $5.4\cdot10^8$), and the error of
   Theorem 10.2 is $0.404\beta^4$. An explicit bound $E(\beta)$ ($\log(V_2/\Upsilon)\le\mathcal T'_p(2+3a+3a^2)$, $-\log(L_2/\Upsilon^2)\le B/(1-B)$, $\Upsilon\ge\Upsilon_+\ge1$) is $\beta^5$ times an
   increasing function, so the error is at most $0.41\beta^4$ for all $\beta<\beta_0(3)$. With Part 14: **for $SU(3)$ in $d=3$,
   $-2.56\beta^4\le m(\mu_\beta)-4\log(1/u)+\log\Upsilon\le0.41\beta^4$ on the whole range**, so the third-order term $\beta^3/432\ge5.75\beta^4$ is determined from both sides, to 45% at
   $\beta_0(3)$ and 1.1% at $10^{-5}$ (Remark 16.9). In $d=4$, with counts to 8 plaquettes, the upper bound reaches $8.2\cdot10^{-5}$, so there both sides hold for $\beta\le1.2\cdot10^{-5}$.
4. *What failed first.* The first estimate used $\theta=x/(1-x)$ in the tails, as Lemma 5.5 does; then $q\approx0.07$ at $\beta_0(3)$ and the tail beyond 10 plaquettes alone was
   $700\beta^4$. The sharper ratio bound was the missing step.
