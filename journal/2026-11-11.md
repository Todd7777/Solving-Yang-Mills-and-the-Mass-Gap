## Part 6: the second-order coefficient ("keep going on the second-order coefficient")

### Goal
Open thread 1 of Part 5: determine and prove the second-order term of $m(\mu_\beta)-4\log(1/u(\beta))$. After Section 9 the lower bound lost
$\delta_\chi\approx128\beta^2$ ($SU(2)$, $d=4$), almost all of it the shifts and jumps of the tube (junctions of two plaquettes) at their sup norm, and the
upper bound had $D^\chi=\frac38\beta^2$ for $SU(2)$, of which $\frac14\beta^2$ came from the crude treatment of the mean.

### The answer, found numerically first (commit `6f774d7`)
* **The quantity.** $\Upsilon(\beta)$, the largest eigenvalue of $\mathsf V=P_\rho(M_{1+f_\beta}-|f_\beta\rangle\langle f_\beta|)P_\rho$ on the span of
  $\chi_\rho,\chi_{\bar\rho}$. Equivalently, the largest variance, under the one-plaquette density $w_\beta/c_0$, of a real unit plaquette function in that
  span ($\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\chi_\rho$ or $\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_\rho$; $\chi_\rho$ if $\rho\cong\bar\rho$).
* **Conjecture, then theorem:** $m=4\log(1/u)-\log\Upsilon+O(\beta^3)$.
* **Exact spectra** (`theory/checks/second-order/exact_spectra.py`):
  * $\mathbb Z_n$ tori in $d=3$, winding zero, both $C$-sectors: agreement up to $-0.02u^3$ at $u\approx2.5\cdot10^{-3}$, i.e. $O(u^4)$.
  * One square (class functions; $SU(2)$, $S_3$, $Q_8$): agreement to all printed digits.
  * Two squares sharing an edge (full link space; $S_3$: $6^7$, $Q_8$: $8^7$ states): the symmetric and antisymmetric plaquette states are degenerate
    and agree with the formula up to $3\cdot10^{-4}\beta^3$. So the shift of the tube does not contribute at second order.
* **A wrong turn.** I first predicted that the shifts lower the $\mathbb Z_2$ gap by about $8u^2$ (eight neighbours, hopping amplitude $u$). The exact
  spectrum says $+u^2$, with a flat band.
  * The resolution: the plaquette–plaquette transfer element is $u^6$ for every pair of plaquettes, adjacent or not. It is exactly the "Ursell"
    product of closing the tube on $q$ and opening it on $q'$, which the subtraction of the vacuum cancels.
  * In the duplicated system this becomes Lemma 10.5: the operator of a shift or jump has rank one and is even under the exchange of the copies.
* **Values** (Weyl integration, `lambda_values.py`): $-\log\Upsilon=$
  * $\beta^2/8$ for $SU(2)$;
  * $\beta^2/8$ for $U(1)$, where the $C$-odd state is the lighter one (the $C$-even one has $3\beta^2/8$);
  * $-\beta/6+\beta^2/72$ for $SU(3)$;
  * $-\beta^2/128$ for $SU(4)$ ($\langle\chi^4\rangle=1$: four fundamental fluxes meet);
  * $O(\beta^3)$ for $SU(N)$, $N\ge5$.

  For $SU(2)$: $m=4\log(4/\beta)+\frac7{24}\beta^2+O(\beta^3)$.

### What was established (Part IV, new Section 10, commit `6df8dcb`; 53 pages)
* **Lemma 10.1 (the plaquette variance).**
  * $\mathsf V$ is the variance form. Its eigenvectors are $\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\chi_\rho$ and $\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_\rho$, with
    $\Upsilon_+=1+\kappa-2N^2u^2$ and $\Upsilon_-=1+\alpha_\rho-\gamma_\rho$.
  * A moment formula to $O(\beta^3)$, and the values above. $\langle\chi^N\rangle=1$ for $SU(N)$ by the invariant-tensor argument of Section 9.
* **Upper bound (Theorem 10.2).** The Section 5 argument with $A=\varphi(U_{p_0})$, $\varphi$ the top eigenvector of $\mathsf V$, and the mean subtracted
  exactly.
  * It needs the cube identity $\int a(U_{p_0})a(U_{p_1})\prod f_\beta=\sum_{r\ne0}|\hat a_r|^2u_r^4$ for real class functions that are not
    inversion invariant: one of the two faces is inverted.
  * $P_\rho\tilde a=\mathsf V\varphi+(\langle f\varphi\rangle-a)P_\rho f$, and so $D_2=\log(V_2/L_2)=-\log\Upsilon+O(\beta^5)$.
* **Lower bound (Theorem 10.8)**, four new ingredients:
  1. *Summing the caps* (Lemma 10.3). Removing all caps is a bijection onto sets without caps plus a choice of caps on the untouched plaquette
     fronts, and it does not change $r_X$. The sum over caps turns the tube $G_g^{\otimes\partial q}$ into the dressed tube
     $D_q=(G_g^{1/2})^{\otimes4}M_{1+g_q}(G_g^{1/2})^{\otimes4}$.
  2. *The swap sectors* (Lemma 10.4). $S$ commutes with every slice operator except those of the slices $0$ and $t$, which anticommute. An empty
     layer carries the projection onto the constants, which is swap-even. So between the observables only the odd sector $P_-$ contributes, on the
     inner slices if an outer layer is empty, and vice versa.
  3. *Odd-sector norms.*
     * The shifts and jumps (Lemma 10.5): $P^JO_JP^J=|\xi\rangle\langle\xi'|$ with $\xi,\xi'$ swap-even, so they vanish on $P_-$. Their full norm is
       $2\sigma_6+\sigma_6^2$.
     * The dressed tube (Lemma 10.6): on the odd sector its $\rho$-block is exactly $u^4\mathsf V$, since the copy switch $vv^{\mathsf T}$ enters with the
       sign $-1$. With the couplings, its norm is $\le u^4\bar\Upsilon=u^4\Upsilon(1+O(\beta^6))$.
  4. *Bookkeeping.*
     * A slice on the odd sector (Lemma 10.7): one "main" piece gets its odd-sector weight $w_\varpi$; every other piece gets its full norm times
       $C_\varpi=1+\max(1/\varpi,(1+\gamma_g)/\bar\Upsilon)$. The odd sector of a tensor product is a direct sum over sign patterns with product $-1$.
     * The path recursion: $z=1/(a^4\bar\Upsilon(1+\delta'))$ is charged only to the slices that the path reaches for the first time.
     * At a slice reached again, the pieces are not main. They cost $\omega=C_\varpi a^4(1+\gamma_g)=O(\beta^3)$ each, which pays for the $C_\varpi$.
     * The induction runs over $\mathcal V(p,D)$, with $D$ the depth of the starting slice below the highest slice reached so far.
     * Four hypotheses: $\Sigma^{(2)}_4<\bar\Upsilon\delta'$, the exit condition with $\bar\Upsilon(1+\delta')-a$, an "old-slice" condition
       $\omega+C_\varpi a^4(2\bar\Upsilon(1+\delta')+\Sigma_4)/(1-\omega)\le1$, and $a^4\bar\Upsilon(1+\delta')\le\varpi$ (for new slices above $t$).
     * $\Sigma^{(2)}_4$ has no cap term, and the shift/jump term is $\varpi\min(b^2,b_2)=O(\beta^3)$ with $\varpi=b$.
     * Spatial sides $\ge5$: a straight four-cycle front is not dressed and would carry $1/(\bar\Upsilon(1+\delta'))$ per slice.
* **Corollary 10.9.** $m(\mu_\beta)=4\log(1/u)-\log\Upsilon+O(\beta^3)$, also in finite volume (sides $\ge5$), with the values above.

### Numbers (Numerical: `theory/checks/second-order/second_order_constants.py`)

  * Near $\beta_0$: $-1.25\cdot10^{-4}$ at $\beta_0(4)$ (Section 9: $-1.28\cdot10^{-4}$) and $-2.38\cdot10^{-4}$ at $\beta_0(3)$ (Section 9: $-2.44\cdot10^{-4}$).

### Checks (`theory/checks/second-order/`, a compass)
* `lemma_checks.py`, by brute force:
  * The cube identity for non-inversion-invariant real class functions ($\mathbb Z_3$, $\mathbb Z_5$, $A_4$): machine precision. The other face

  * The dressed tube on the swap-odd sector ($\mathbb Z_3$, $\mathbb Z_4$, $S_3$; full two-copy space of four links, Lanczos): between $u^4\Upsilon$ and

### What failed / limitations
* **The shift prediction** ($-8u^2$ for $\mathbb Z_2$) was wrong; see above.
* **The first draft of the path recursion was wrong.**
  * A per-slice union bound over the regions covered more than once by the path does not converge (ratio $>1+\delta'$). Hence the recursion that
    charges $z$ only to newly reached slices.
  * The draft also had a wrong prefactor ($a^{|p|/2}$ instead of $a^{|p|}$ for a segment ending at an old slice) and missed two hypotheses: the
    old-slice condition, and the new slices above $t$, where only a full-norm bound is available. Both were found when writing out the recursion
    in full, before the referee.
* **The parameter clashed with the paper's notation:** it was called $\epsilon$, which is $e^{2\beta}-1$ there; it is now $\varpi$.
* **The first run of the cube-identity check** included the trivial representation (with $u_0=1$) in the sum; the identity, like Lemma 5.1(ii), sums over
  $r\ne0$. The text now says so explicitly.
* **Large constant.** The lower bound's $O(\beta^3)$ has a constant of about $10^6$ ($d=4$). The term $\beta^2/8$ is resolved only for $\beta\lesssim10^{-7}$.
  The new content is the asymptotic statement; on the whole range it is only marginally better than Section 9.
* **Only the lowest mass is bounded.** The $C$-odd mass of $SU(3)$ ($-\log\Upsilon_-=+\beta/6+O(\beta^2)$) remains Heuristic.

### Open threads after Part 6
1. **Third order.**
   * The constant of the lower bound comes from crude counts: the junctions with three plaquettes and a four-edge exit (the enumerations of Section 8
     found none, so a proof that $Z_{3,4}=0$ would remove the largest term), the junctions with three or more front-pairs, and the six-edge exits.
   * Evaluating these by characters would give the $\beta^3$ coefficient.
2. **The $C$-odd sector.** A lower bound for $C$-odd observables would prove the $SU(3)$ splitting.
   * $C$ commutes with the transfer matrix and with the exchange of the copies, so the odd–odd sector of the duplicated system is the natural place.
3. **Near $\beta_0(d)$.** $\mathcal T_Q$ (upper bound) and the crude counts of Section 8 (lower bound) still dominate.
