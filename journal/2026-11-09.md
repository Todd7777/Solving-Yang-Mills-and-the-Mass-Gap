### Part II: `theory/infinite-volume-strong-coupling.tex` (new, 20 pages; refereed and revised)
"Lattice Yang–Mills theory at strong coupling II: the infinite-volume theory, its transfer operator and its mass gap".
Complete proofs, in the framework of the foundations monograph [F]:

* **Every $\beta$.** Limit points of the periodic Wilson measures are translation-invariant Gibbs measures (DLR equations
  proved by lifting the torus specification to $\mathbb Z^d$); along even time extents they are reflection positive, and
  lattice OS reconstruction gives a Hilbert space and a self-adjoint contraction $\hat T_\mu$, so the **infinite-volume mass
  gap $m(\mu)$ is defined at every coupling**. This makes the infinite-volume form of (G1) precise: $m(\mu)>0$ for every
  limit point (docs/03 §3.2, "(G1$_\infty$)"). Elitzur's theorem in infinite volume (links of any finite forest are i.i.d.
  Haar) holds for every Gibbs measure at every $\beta$.
* **A sharp total-variation lemma**: densities with ratio in $[ce^{-\delta},ce^{\delta}]$ are at distance
  $\le\tanh(\delta/2)$ (sharp). For the Wilson specification this gives Dobrushin coefficients $\le\tanh\beta$ instead
  of $e^{4\beta}-1$, so the strong-coupling threshold improves from $\beta_D(d)=\frac14\log(1+\frac1{6(d-1)})$ to
  $\beta_*(d)=\operatorname{artanh}\frac1{6(d-1)}$ (factor $\approx4$; $\beta_*(4)\approx0.0556$). This also improves the
  volume-uniform finite-volume gap of [F]: $m_{\Lambda_s}(\beta)\ge\log(1/(6(d-1)\tanh\beta))$.
* **For $\beta<\beta_*(d)$:** unique Gibbs measure; periodic, free and fixed boundary conditions all converge with an
  explicit rate $\alpha^{L/2}$; invariance under the hypercubic group and *all* gauge transformations; exponential
  clustering; the **global Markov property with respect to time slices** (from a boundary-influence lemma that tracks
  which boundary links differ, applied to half-boxes); hence the spatial configurations of successive time slices form a
  stationary reversible **Markov chain** whose transition operator $P$ on $L^2(\nu)$ ($\nu$ = time-zero marginal, the
  infinite-volume vacuum measure) is unitarily equivalent to the OS transfer operator; $0\le P\le1$ (from positivity of
  the finite-volume transfer matrices); $\operatorname{spec}P\subseteq[0,6(d-1)\tanh\beta]\cup\{1\}$, unique vacuum, and
  $m(\mu_\beta)=-\log(1-\operatorname{gap}P)\ge\log(1/(6(d-1)\tanh\beta))$. Gauge-variant states lie in $\ker P$.
* So the exact Markov-chain reformulation of the gap (docs/03 §3.5) holds **in infinite volume**, not only on finite
  lattices. Area law for the unique measure (with Part III).
* **Referee:** an independent referee checked every proof (with computer checks of the torus lifting, the half-box
  geometry, the Dobrushin coefficients and the TV lemma): every Theorem/Proposition/Lemma/Corollary correct, complete
  proofs; minor fixes applied (RP only along even time extents; $U(1)$ hedging; what the Markov property adds; a
  measurability sentence; notation $E^{(0)}$ for the time-zero slice).

### Part IV: `theory/mass-gap-strong-coupling.tex` (new, 7 pages; referee check started)
"Lattice Yang–Mills theory at strong coupling IV: the mass gap with the correct leading asymptotics". For $d\ge3$ and
$\beta<\beta_0(d)$: $m(\mu_\beta)$ and the finite-volume gaps are $\ge4\log(1/q(\beta))$, $q=e^3K(e^{4\beta}-1)$, i.e.
$4\log(1/\beta)-O(1)$ — **the leading coefficient 4 of the strong-coupling expansion** (the minimal glueball is a tube with
four plaquettes per unit time). This is principle 3 ("the strong-coupling test") of the research program, which the
Dobrushin bounds ($\log(1/\beta)$) do not pass. Proof: duplicate variables (all non-connecting terms cancel by the swap
symmetry), Part III's loop expansion and ratio bound for the duplicated system ($\Xi^2$), and a **layer lemma**: a
surface without free edges has $\ge4$ temporal plaquettes in each time layer it crosses, because those plaquettes form a
graph of minimum degree 2 on the spatial lattice, whose girth is 4. In $d=2$ the girth is the spatial size, which gives
an infinite gap in infinite volume and a finite-volume gap linear in $L_1$, as in the exact solution.

## Honest assessment
Uniqueness and clustering at strong coupling are classical (Dobrushin; Osterwalder–Seiler), and so is the
$-4\log u$ behaviour of the strong-coupling glueball mass. What is new for this project: (i) a complete, self-contained
proof of milestone T1.1 for a range four times larger than [F]'s; (ii) the identification of the infinite-volume
transfer operator with a reversible Markov chain of time slices, which turns the exact reformulation of docs/03 §3.5
into a statement about the infinite-volume theory; (iii) a definition of the infinite-volume mass gap at every coupling,
so the open problem has a sharp infinite-volume formulation (G1$_\infty$); (iv) a strong-coupling lower bound with the
correct leading asymptotics, so the strong-coupling endpoint now passes the program's strong-coupling test. Every
argument in Parts II–IV applies verbatim to $U(1)$: they are the strong-coupling endpoint, and the crossover (docs/03
§3.4), i.e. the actual Millennium problem, is untouched.
