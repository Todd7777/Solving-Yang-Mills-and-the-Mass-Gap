## What was established

### Part II: `theory/infinite-volume-strong-coupling.tex` (new, 19 pages)
"Lattice Yang–Mills theory at strong coupling II: the infinite-volume theory, its transfer operator and its mass gap".
Complete proofs, in the framework of the foundations monograph [F]:

* **Every $\beta$.** Limit points of the periodic Wilson measures (even time extents) are translation- and
  reflection-invariant, reflection-positive Gibbs measures (DLR equations proved by lifting the torus specification
  to $\mathbb Z^d$). Lattice OS reconstruction gives a Hilbert space and a self-adjoint contraction $\hat T_\mu$, so the
  **infinite-volume mass gap $m(\mu)$ is defined at every coupling**. This makes the infinite-volume form of (G1)
  precise: $m(\mu)>0$ for every limit point (docs/03 §3.2, "(G1$_\infty$)").
* **A sharp total-variation lemma**: densities with ratio in $[ce^{-\delta},ce^{\delta}]$ are at distance
  $\le\tanh(\delta/2)$ (sharp). For the Wilson specification this gives Dobrushin coefficients $\le\tanh\beta$ instead
  of $e^{4\beta}-1$, so the strong-coupling threshold improves from $\beta_D(d)=\frac14\log(1+\frac1{6(d-1)})$ to
  $\beta_*(d)=\operatorname{artanh}\frac1{6(d-1)}$ (factor $\approx4$; $\beta_*(4)\approx0.0556$). This also improves the
  volume-uniform finite-volume gap of [F]: $m_{\Lambda_s}(\beta)\ge\log(1/(6(d-1)\tanh\beta))$.
* **For $\beta<\beta_*(d)$:** unique Gibbs measure; periodic, free and fixed boundary conditions all converge with an
  explicit rate $\alpha^{L/2}$; invariance under the hypercubic group and *all* gauge transformations (so the links of
  any finite forest are i.i.d. Haar: Elitzur in infinite volume); exponential clustering; the **global Markov property
  with respect to time slices** (proved from a boundary-influence lemma that tracks which boundary links differ,
  applied to half-boxes); hence the spatial configurations of successive time slices form a stationary reversible
  **Markov chain** whose transition operator $P$ on $L^2(\nu)$ ($\nu$ = time-zero marginal, the infinite-volume vacuum
  measure) is unitarily equivalent to the OS transfer operator; $0\le P\le1$ (from positivity of the finite-volume
  transfer matrices); $\operatorname{spec}P\subseteq[0,6(d-1)\tanh\beta]\cup\{1\}$, unique vacuum, and
  $m(\mu_\beta)=-\log(1-\operatorname{gap}P)\ge\log(1/(6(d-1)\tanh\beta))$. Gauge-variant states lie in $\ker P$.
* So the exact Markov-chain reformulation of the gap (docs/03 §3.5) holds **in infinite volume**, not only on finite
  lattices. Area law for the unique measure (with Part III).
* Status: Theorem-level, complete proofs. An independent referee check was started; see "What failed" below.

### Part III revised after two independent referee reports
Both referees (one focused on the chain/selection-rule/counting machinery, one on the analytic estimates and the
literature) found **no error in any Theorem, Proposition, Lemma or Corollary**; they verified the constants, the chain
identities by exact integer computation, Prop. `al:prop:area` by integer programming (including wrapping cases), the
rooted-tree encoding by brute force, and the literature claims against the arXiv texts. Defects were in the abstract,
introduction and remarks, and are fixed:
* "every non-trivial Wilson loop decays exponentially in its perimeter" is false for general closed paths (a loop
  traversed twice); it is now stated and **proved for rectangular loops in every dimension for all $\beta<\beta_0(d)$**
  (new $d=3$ and $d=2$ computations; the old proof covered only $d\ge4$).
* The heuristic solid-torus construction for $N$-ality zero needs $L_\mu\ge R+3$ (at $R+2$ it is a genus-2
  handlebody), and placing the fundamental on its boundary works for the adjoint only.
* Literature attributions corrected (Fernández–Procacci §1 vs §2; Cao–Nissim–Sheffield Remark 1.5); the $U(1)_4$
  statement hedged (Fröhlich–Spencer prove the Villain case and assert Wilson's).
* Smaller: $\beta_0(d)$ is a convergence threshold, not "the radius"; the leading term is $X=D$ only when
  $RT\le L_\mu L_\nu-RT$; notation ($\mathbb Z_M$-chains renamed $\zeta$).

## Honest assessment
Nothing here is new mathematics at the level of the literature: uniqueness and clustering at strong coupling are
classical (Dobrushin; Osterwalder–Seiler). What is new for this project is (i) a complete, self-contained proof of
milestone T1.1 for a range four times larger than [F]'s Dobrushin range, (ii) the identification of the
infinite-volume transfer operator with a reversible Markov chain of time slices, which turns the exact reformulation
of docs/03 §3.5 into a statement about the infinite-volume theory, and (iii) a precise definition of the
infinite-volume mass gap at every coupling, so that the open problem has a sharp infinite-volume formulation
(G1$_\infty$). Every argument in Parts II and III applies verbatim to $U(1)$: they are the strong-coupling endpoint,
and the crossover (docs/03 §3.4) is untouched.
